The issue

Business users and AI agents both need governed access to the same data, without a separate trust model per surface.

Business users

Ask plain-English questions, build dashboards, and work through Genie, copilots, and agents. Each expects to see only the data their own permissions allow.

AI agents

Orchestrate tools and call data on a user's behalf. Partners and customers need the same capabilities, reached through their own portals and their own identity providers.

Same data, same AI, same governance expected, whether the requester is an employee or an external partner or customer authenticating through their own IdP.
What: the model, at a glance

One authorization engine, however authentication arrives

Databricks enforces authorization in exactly one place: Unity Catalog. Authentication can originate from the workspace identity provider for internal users, or from an external IdP via token federation for partners and customers. Every path converges on UC before any data or tool is touched.

Same governance, regardless of which door the identity walked in. Data, tools, and agents all check the same UC privileges.

What: token paths

Three token paths, one identity model

U2M, OBO, and M2M are token acquisition paths, not separate authentication systems. What changes is whose identity Unity Catalog sees.

PathWho authenticatesIdentity in UC
U2MThe human, directlyThe human
OBOThe human, via an app that forwards the tokenThe human
M2MThe application itself (service principal)The service principal
Human calling directly: U2M. Human going through an app: OBO. App itself with no human present: M2M.
What: enforcement layers

Seven layers, checked on every call

LayerGoverns
1 · IdentityAgent service principal, user OBO, token federation

No design should rely on a single layer. Service Policies are the superset governing content and tool execution.

MCP Services and model services are UC securables with governed tags, role-based access, and service policies. PostgreSQL (Lakebase) uses native PG authorization (roles, GRANT, RLS) — the exception to the UC model.

Why this matters

One model, not one per surface

Consistent governance

The same rules apply whether the surface is Genie, Teams, a custom app, or a partner portal. No per-integration reinterpretation of who can see what.

Lower audit burden

One control plane to prove compliance against: system.access.audit and MLflow traces cover every surface, not a bespoke log per integration.

Velocity

New AI surfaces plug into the same identity and authorization model instead of re-solving it, so teams ship faster without reopening the trust question.
